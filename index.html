What this is. A prediction model for the 2026 season, plus the tools to argue with it. It rates every team from play-by-play data, projects each game, and keeps score of how it does — against the closing line, against simple baselines, and against you.

How to use it. Today is the Sunday-morning view: what's on, what's worth watching, where your picks stand. Slate is where you dig into a game, nudge the model with what it can't see, and lock a pick. The rest — schedule, standings, playoff scenarios, a territory map, a survivor solver, daily trivia and season stats — are there when you want them.

What the model can't see this week

  • No 2026 starters are declared yet, so every quarterback is carried over from last season. Free agency, the draft, and camp battles are all invisible.
  • Injury reports don't exist until games are played. Availability is zeroed for all 16 games.
  • Treat large disagreements with the market as the model missing personnel news, not as an edge.

We beat the naive baselines comfortably and lose to the betting market. That gap is honest and it is the thing we're trying to close.
Seeds come from resolving every unforced game to its favourite, so this is the bracket if the rest of the season plays to form. Reseeding follows the real rule: the top surviving seed always draws the lowest one left. Changing an earlier round clears the rounds after it.
Forced results are treated as certain; every other game is simulated from the model's win probability. Tiebreakers follow the NFL ladder through strength of schedule and reproduce the actual playoff field for all six seasons since the 14-team format began.
Week 18
Each county is assigned to the team whose stadium is nearest, which stands in for TV market boundaries — Nielsen's actual designated market areas are licensed data we don't reproduce. Shared markets are split with offset centers, so the New York and Los Angeles pairs each divide their metro.
One team per week, never twice, and a loss ends your entry. The best remaining path is solved exactly — assigning teams to weeks to maximise the product of win probabilities, which is a linear assignment problem, not a week-by-week greedy choice. The percentage beside each team is what taking it now costs your remaining path; picking the strongest available team is often not optimal because it spends that team when a later week needs it more.
One question a day. Every question is generated from the same tables this model is built on — opponent-adjusted EPA splits, 2025 results, and our own validation output — so each answer comes with the numbers behind it rather than a claim you have to take on faith.

Appearance

Ridge model over Kalman-filtered team strength, opponent-adjusted EPA splits, quarterback rating, per-stadium home field, and personnel availability.
